Our story

Small hives, big skies, honest honey.

The Cumberland Honey Co. began with a single hive at the bottom of a garden and a lot of curiosity. A few seasons (and a few stings) later, we look after colonies spread across the northern edge of the Lake District, from the Solway plain to the high heather moors.

Every jar we sell comes from our own bees. We don't buy in, blend, or heat our honey. That means each harvest tastes a little different, depending on the weather, the flowers and the hives it came from. We think that's the whole point.

Beyond the honey, we're here to look after bees. We work with local farmers and landowners to plant forage, leave field margins wild, and keep our colonies healthy without cutting corners.

"Look after the bees, and the honey looks after itself."

Raw & real

Gently strained and jarred straight from the comb. No heating, no filtering out the good bits, nothing added.

Rooted in Cumbria

Every hive lives within the old county of Cumberland, so every jar tells you exactly where it's from.

Bees come first

We only take surplus honey, always leaving the bees plenty to see them through a long northern winter.

Meet the bees

The real workers behind every jar

Our colonies are hardy, dark, locally-adapted honey bees, well suited to Cumbrian weather: they fly in cooler temperatures, winter well and make the most of a short northern summer.

A year in the hive

Mar – May

Spring build-up

The queen ramps up laying, colonies grow fast on willow, blossom and dandelion, and we watch closely for swarming.

Jun – Jul

Summer flow

Meadows are in full bloom and foragers bring in nectar by the bucketload. Our first honey harvest happens now.

Aug – Sep

Heather & harvest

Hives head up to the moors for heather. Back home, we extract, strain and jar the year's honey by hand.

Oct – Feb

Winter cluster

The bees huddle together on their stores to keep warm. We leave them in peace, and render wax for candles.
